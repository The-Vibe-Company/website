To choose an AI agency, a freelancer or an IT services firm, ask every candidate for the same 8 documents, in writing and in the same words, then check them yourself. On November 12, 2026, the editorial team reviewed 27 AI provider websites, and only 2 of them say how they manage their subcontractors. You have to ask for the rest in writing. For each document, this guide gives the text behind it (French Labor Code, GDPR, the 1975 law) and the official register where you can check it.

## Ready-made software before any AI provider

In France, companies that use AI rely first on ready-made software, according to [Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/isoc_eb_ai/default/table?lang=en). The figures cover 2025 and companies with at least 10 people, excluding agriculture, mining and the financial sector.

| Company size | Ready-made software | External providers | Own employees |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 to 49 people | 78% | 23% | 19% |
| 50 to 249 people | 83% | 31% | 20% |
| 250 people or more | 87% | 49% | 38% |

The modes add up, since 78 + 23 + 19 already exceeds 100% for companies with 10 to 49 people: a company can combine several. For an SME that already uses AI, the most common option is ready-made software. The first question of the choice then becomes simple. Does an existing tool do the job?

The MIT NANDA report, *The GenAI Divide*, finds that in its sample, external partnerships with learning-capable, customized tools reached deployment about 67% of the time, compared with about 33% for internally built tools, according to the [primary report](https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf). These self-reported results come from 52 organizations, and the report states that the correlation does not prove causation. The same report sees millions in value in only 5% of integrated pilots. The vast majority remain with no measurable P&L impact.

Three questions then separate the options: what costs more, failing or waiting; is the need defined or still to invent; who will own the system in a year. To see which option fits which project, the comparison [AI agency vs freelancer vs IT services firm](/en/resources/articles/ai-agency-vs-freelancer-vs-it-services) covers each one and its costs.

## The kit: 8 documents to ask every AI provider for

The kit comes down to 8 documents, each with the text or rule behind it, where to check it and the warning sign to watch for. This table does not replace legal advice, and each legal consequence is to be checked by a lawyer.

| Document to ask for | Text or rule | Where to check it | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. K or K bis extract, RNE extract or a document with the registration number | French Labor Code, D8222-5: the client obtains this document at signing, then every six months | On the document itself, dated | No registration number |
| 2. Urssaf vigilance certificate less than six months old | L8222-1 and D8222-5: check at signing, then every six months; mandatory from EUR 5,000 excl. VAT in total, according to Urssaf | Urssaf verification tool, with the 15-character security code | L8222-2 makes a client who did not check jointly liable, in case of recorded undeclared work |
| 3. Named list of subcontractors | Law 75-1334, art. 3: the contractor has each subcontractor accepted by the project owner; Urssaf: the same checks at the second tier | Written list, then each subcontractor's vigilance certificate | No list of subcontractors |
| 4. GDPR guarantees and where the data is hosted | GDPR, Art. 28(1): the client uses only processors "providing sufficient guarantees" | Written answers to the data and security questions | No answer on hosting or on data sent to the models |
| 5. Certificates the provider claims | An ISO/IEC 27001, HDS or SecNumCloud certificate attests a security level without being a GDPR certification; GDPR Art. 28(5) refers to a certification mechanism approved "as referred to in Article 42", which "may be used as an element" to demonstrate those guarantees | IAF CertSearch for an [ISO/IEC 27001](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001) certificate, ANS list for an HDS host, ANSSI list for SecNumCloud | Certificate not found in the register |
| 6. References in production | Method rule: time in production, number of users, a usage figure, a client contact to call | Call the client contact | Logos only, no project in production |
| 7. Complete proposal | Method rule: problem and metric, scope and out of scope, data, architecture and recurring costs, phases, acceptance testing, named team, security and subcontractors, ownership, what comes next | Read it point by point | A missing point, with no answer when you ask |
| 8. A POC that decides | Method rule: one question, real data, users, a numeric criterion set in advance | In your environment, on your data | A POC that only runs on the provider's laptop |

According to Urssaf, the vigilance certificate is mandatory from EUR 5,000 excl. VAT on the total value of the service, even when paid in several invoices. The check therefore applies to a freelancer too once the contract reaches that amount, which a scoping phase followed by phases often does. When the provider hands part of the development to a freelancer or another company, the client can ask for the named list of subcontractors before signing. Whether the 1975 law applies exactly to an IT service is to be checked by a lawyer.

An AI development provider generally does not host the data itself. Ask it which host, and which HDS activities or which qualified service, cover the project's data, then look that host up in the official list. For health data, the guide [AI and health data](/en/resources/articles/ai-health-data-hds-france) explains what HDS requires.

A proposal split into phases should include an explicit stop point after the first phase. To judge the POC itself, the [POC to production checklist](/en/resources/articles/ai-agent-poc-to-production) gives the criteria. Once you have chosen the provider, [working with an AI agency](/en/resources/articles/working-with-an-ai-development-agency) covers the project stages and the contract clauses.

### The written request to send every candidate

Copy this block and send it in the same words to every candidate.

```markdown
Subject: verification file before signing

Please attach to your proposal:
1. A K or K bis extract, an RNE extract or a document with your registration number.
2. An Urssaf vigilance certificate less than six months old.
3. The named list of your subcontractors on this project.
4. Where the data will be hosted and your GDPR guarantees (Article 28).
5. The certificates you claim, with their scope and date.
6. References in production, with a client contact to call.
7. A proposal that covers: problem and metric, scope and out of scope, data, architecture and recurring costs, phases, acceptance testing, named team, security and subcontractors, ownership, what comes next.
8. The proposed POC: question, data, users, numeric criterion set in advance.

Please also answer these 10 questions:
1. Which projects have you put into production, and how long have they been running?
2. Who, by name, will work on this project?
3. What is the first usable result, and when?
4. How do you measure the AI's quality, and who signs off?
5. What happens to the project's data (hosting, models, logs, tests)?
6. What rights will the AI have in the company's tools?
7. What recurring costs should be planned?
8. Who owns the code, the prompts, the tests and the documentation?
9. How does handover or exit work?
10. What happens if the criteria are not met after the first phase?
```

## AI provider websites: what 27 of them publish

On November 12, 2026, the editorial team listed 30 AI providers from 3 web searches, 4 Ubersuggest results pages and 2 directories (impli.fr, cartelis.com). It analyzed 27 sites, because 2 were unreachable and it excluded 1 for a conflict of interest. For each site, the review covered the home page and up to 15 internal pages.

| What the site publishes | Sites | Share |
|---|---|---|
| At least one service price (audit, POC, sprint, day of work, training) | 9 of 27 | 33% |
| Where client or product data is hosted | 7 of 27 | 26% |
| How it manages its subcontractors (Art. 28 contract or published list) | 2 of 27 | 7% |
| At least one AI provider or model named (automatic detection) | 23 of 27 | 85% |

What Article 28(1) asks you to check, guarantees, subcontractors, hosting, is therefore almost never online. You have to ask every candidate for it in writing. The sample, small and not random, gives an order of magnitude and does not count as a market statistic. A single person coded the sites, and the shares are lower bounds, because information published elsewhere on a site may have slipped past the review. The detection of named models is automatic, with no site-by-site review, and this count names no provider. The agences-ia.fr barometer, for its part, finds published prices at 81% of 57 agencies, with a different corpus and a different definition.

## Warning signs before you sign with an AI provider

- Results guaranteed before anyone has seen your data.
- No project in production to show, only demos.
- A scoping phase of several months with nothing to test.
- A pitch focused on tools.
- Certifications or labels you can't verify, or a certificate missing from the register.
- No list of subcontractors.
- No clear answer on code ownership or on exit.

## FAQ

**Should you ask a freelancer for a vigilance certificate?**
Yes, according to Urssaf, once the contract reaches EUR 5,000 excl. VAT in total. The French Labor Code covers any person who signs the contract, whatever the provider's status. Ask for the certificate at signing and then every six months, and check it with its 15-character code. This point remains to be checked by a lawyer.

**Is an ISO/IEC 27001 certification enough for the GDPR?**
No. It attests a security level, but it is not a GDPR certification. Article 28(5) refers to a certification mechanism approved "as referred to in Article 42", which "may be used as an element" to demonstrate the guarantees. First check the certificate in IAF CertSearch, which verifies that it is valid and that the certification body was accredited. Then ask for the guarantees in writing, before signing.

**How do you check that a host is on the HDS list?**
Look the host up in the list that ANS publishes on esante.gouv.fr. For each host, the list gives the HDS activities covered, the version of the standard and the certification body. Also ask the provider which host and which HDS activities cover the project's data, since it generally does not host the data itself.

**Can the agency subcontract to freelancers?**
If it does, the 1975 law provides, for a works contract, that the contractor has each subcontractor accepted by the project owner. Ask for the named list before signing, then each subcontractor's vigilance certificate. Whether this law applies to an IT service is to be checked by a lawyer.

**Agency or in-house team?**
First check that ready-made software does not do the job, since it is the most common mode among French companies that use AI, according to Eurostat. In the MIT NANDA sample, external partnerships reach deployment about 67% of the time, compared with 33% in-house, with no proof of causation. The comparison linked above covers the rest.
