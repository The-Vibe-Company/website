Anyone can build an AI demo. A tool your teams still use six months later is a lot rarer.

That's why "agency, IT services firm, freelancer or in-house?" is often the wrong framing. People compare rates and résumés. The real difference is elsewhere: who can get the project into production, and who'll keep it running.

MIT's *The GenAI Divide* report (Project NANDA, 2025) made headlines: it found that 95% of the generative AI pilots it studied showed no measurable P&L return. The methodology has been debated. But if you've watched a proof of concept die in a drawer, it rings true: the problem is rarely the model. It's getting to real use.

We're an AI agency, so we're not neutral. We'll still try to be honest, including about when an agency is the wrong choice.

## The real question: who will carry the project into production?

1. **Is the project strategic and long-lived?** If AI becomes the core of your product, you'll eventually want in-house skills.
2. **Is the need well defined?** A precise task needs something different from a product still to be invented.
3. **Who will maintain it?** An AI agent is never "done": models, data and usage all change.

## The four options at a glance

| | In-house team | Freelancer | IT services firm | AI agency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time to start | slow (hiring) | fast | medium | fast |
| AI specialization | depends on hires | strong on one topic | varies by profile | core business |
| Full product (front, back, infra) | if the team is complete | rarely alone | yes | yes, if the team is cross-functional |
| Scaling headcount | slow | limited | strong | medium |
| Knowledge of your business | best | to build | to build | to build |
| Main risk | delays, retention | single-person dependency | interchangeable profiles | dependency if nothing is handed over |

## When to choose an in-house team

If AI is core to your competitive edge and you have time to hire, in-house is the goal. Nobody will know your data and users better. The trap: hiring before validating a first use case.

## When to choose a freelancer

A good freelancer is unbeatable on a precise task: a connector, an automation, a technical audit. The limits show when the project needs several skills at once (interface, backend, infrastructure, security) or continuity if that person becomes unavailable.

## When to choose an IT services firm

These firms can staff people at scale and fit into your procurement and security processes. They're a good choice to reinforce an existing team on a known scope. Check who'll actually be on the project, and what AI they've really shipped to production.

## When to choose an AI agency

An agency makes sense when you want to go fast from idea to a tool in production, with a small team covering the whole path: scoping, product, development, deployment. It's also a good way to validate a use case before bringing it in-house. One condition: the agency has to prove it ships to production, and plan the handover.

## 10 questions to ask before you sign

1. **What have you put into production, and how long has it been running?** Ask for comparable cases and references you can call.
2. **Who exactly will work on my project?**
3. **What first usable result, and when?**
4. **How do you measure AI quality?** Test sets, real cases, success criteria set up front.
5. **What happens to our data?** Hosting, subprocessors, model calls, logs, test environments.
6. **What rights will the AI have?** Read, propose, prepare or execute aren't the same risk.
7. **What recurring costs should we plan for?** Models, hosting, maintenance.
8. **Who owns the code, prompts and documentation?**
9. **How does the exit work?** Handover, documentation, reversibility.
10. **What if it doesn't work?** A good partner knows when to stop.

## Red flags

- Guaranteed results before they have seen your data.
- No production projects to show, only demos.
- Certifications or labels claimed without proof. Ask for the certificates, their scope and their dates.
- Talk focused on tools rather than your business process.
- No clear answer on code ownership.

## FAQ

### AI agency or IT services firm: what's the difference?

An IT services firm mostly supplies people to reinforce your teams. An AI agency takes a project end to end with its own team, from scoping to production. The line is blurry: judge on delivered projects, not labels.

### Should we start with a proof of concept?

Yes, if it answers a real decision question: which scenario, which data, which users, which expected result. A POC without success criteria rarely reaches production.

### Can we bring it in-house after working with an agency?

Yes, and it's a good path: validate the use case with an agency, then take it over. Plan documentation, code ownership and handover sessions in the contract.

### How do we check an AI agency's references?

Ask for production cases with numbers, client contacts you can call, and check that the products actually exist.
