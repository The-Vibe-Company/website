**The short answer.** AI agency, freelancer or IT services firm: it depends on the project. Hire a freelancer for a precise AI task your team can take over: a connector, an extraction pipeline, an audit. Hire an IT services firm (what the French call an ESN) to add people to a team that already knows what it is building, over the long run. Hire an AI agency when you need a complete product or agent in production, fast, without the team in-house. The right choice depends less on the day rate than on three questions: who owns the outcome, who manages the work day to day, and who takes the system over in a year.

Most comparisons stop at the day rate. It's the least useful number. A €700-a-day freelancer who ships alone in six weeks can cost less than a team billing €1,000 a day. The opposite happens too, when someone has to manage the work and then add the interface, security and infrastructure around it. This guide starts from your project, not from the vendor.

## The comparison in one table

| Criterion | AI freelancer | IT services firm (ESN) | AI agency |
|---|---|---|---|
| What you buy | an expert's time | people, often on time and materials | an outcome delivered by a team |
| Who manages day to day | you | you, or the firm's project lead | the agency, with a contact on your side |
| Start | fast if the person is available | a few weeks (sourcing) | fast |
| Scope covered | one topic, in depth | broad, depending on who is staffed | full product: front end, back end, AI, infra |
| Scaling up | limited to one person | strong | medium |
| Usual pricing | day rate | day rate, sometimes fixed price | fixed price per phase or capped time and materials |
| Main risk | depending on one person | interchangeable profiles, heavy management | lock-in if nothing is handed over |
| Wins when | the need fits in one sentence | volume and duration matter most | you need to reach production fast |

These cells are tendencies, not rules. Some IT services firms are very strong in AI, and some freelancers work in collectives. Judge on shipped projects, not on the label.

## What each option really costs

### Day rates, with their sources

The most solid public numbers are for freelancers in France:

- **Experienced freelance data scientists**: €681 a day on average, per [Malt's data scientist rate barometer](https://www.malt.fr/t/barometre-tarifs/expert-data/data-scientist) (profiles active over the last three months).
- **Experienced freelance developers**: €576 a day on average, per [Malt's developer rate barometer](https://www.malt.fr/t/barometre-tarifs/tech).
- **AI / LLM freelance specialists**: a median of €810 a day, with half of reported rates between €680 and €940, per [TJMètre's AI barometer](https://tjmetre.fr/statistiques-tjm-ia). The sample is small (41 observations), so read it as an order of magnitude.

For IT services firms, [La Fabrique du Net](https://www.lafabriquedunet.fr/agences/pages/agences-esn-ssii/tarifs) reports day rates from €320 to €2,000 depending on the profile, and estimates that a mid-level developer for three months comes to roughly €25,000 to €35,000.

For AI agencies, there is no reliable public benchmark. The ranges you see on blogs come from the agencies themselves. Ask for a price per phase with a deliverable, and compare it with the total cost of the other options.

### Total cost, not the day rate

To compare fairly, add up:

1. **The service**: days × rate, or the price of each phase.
2. **Your management time**: specifying, deciding, testing. On time and materials, that time sits with you, and it is often underestimated.
3. **What is missing around it**: a freelancer who knows models won't necessarily build the interface, authentication, hosting or security. Someone will have to.
4. **Running costs**: model calls, hosting, monitoring.
5. **What comes next**: maintenance, updates when models change, handover.

An AI project is never frozen. Models change every few months, and so do your data and processes. The cheapest option on day one is rarely the cheapest after a year.

## When each option wins

### The freelancer wins when the need fits in one sentence

"Extract amounts and dates from our PDF contracts into our ERP, with an error rate measured on 200 documents." That's a good freelance brief. The scope is clear, so is the success criterion, and someone on your side can run the result.

Freelancers also win on rare expertise: an audit of your RAG architecture, a model evaluation, help on a hard problem for a team that already exists.

It breaks down when the project needs interface, back end, infrastructure and security all at once, or when the person leaves halfway. Require the code in your repository, documentation as you go, and access in your company's name from day one.

### The IT services firm wins when volume and duration matter most

You have a product team and a roadmap, and you're short of hands for eighteen months? An IT services firm can supply several profiles, replace them, and get through your procurement and security questionnaires. That's its job.

The flip side: on time and materials, you manage the work. If the AI need is still fuzzy, you pay for days spent finding out. In France, time and materials also has a legal frame: the labour code bans [any for-profit operation whose sole purpose is lending staff](https://code.travail.gouv.fr/code-du-travail/l8241-1). A sound engagement relies on real expertise and supervision by the vendor, not on consultants directed day to day like employees. Your legal and procurement teams know the topic. Ask them before you sign.

### The AI agency wins when you need production, fast

The typical case: an AI product or agent that has to run inside your tools, with real data, access rights and usage metrics, within a few weeks. Nobody in-house has the time or the full skill set. The agency owns the outcome with a small team that covers the whole path.

The risk: ending up dependent on a system nobody on your side understands. Put it in the contract from the start: ownership of the code, prompts and test sets, and a handover with documentation and runbooks.

## Eight situations, one recommendation

| Your situation | Best-fit option | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A single connector or automation | Freelancer | clear scope, easy takeover |
| An audit or expert opinion on your AI stack | Freelancer | one-off expertise |
| Reinforcing an existing product team for 12+ months | IT services firm | volume, replaceable profiles |
| A program with dozens of developers | IT services firm | capacity, procurement, framework |
| A first AI agent in production in your tools | AI agency | full product, short timeline |
| A new AI product, from mockup to launch | AI agency | cross-functional team |
| Validating a use case before hiring | AI agency, then bring it in-house | learn before you hire |
| Industrializing AI across your own dev teams | AI agency embedded in your teams | standardize tools and practices |

## Combine instead of choosing

The best setups often mix options. An agency builds the first system and trains the team while you hire. A freelancer then takes over maintenance. An IT services firm reinforces the teams once the method is in place.

## The questions that decide it

Before you sign, whatever the option, get written answers to these:

1. Who owns the outcome if the criteria aren't met?
2. Who, by name, works on the project, and for how much time?
3. What is the first usable deliverable, and when?
4. How is the AI's quality measured, and on what data?
5. Who owns the code, prompts, tests and documentation?
6. What happens if the key person leaves?
7. What recurring costs come after delivery?

## FAQ

### AI agency or freelancer: which is cheaper?

Per day, the freelancer almost always is. Per project, it depends on what's left to do around them: management, interface, infrastructure, security, maintenance. Compare the total cost over a year, not the day rate.

### How much does an AI freelancer cost in France?

Per Malt's barometer, an experienced freelance data scientist charges €681 a day on average. TJMètre gives a median of €810 for AI / LLM specialists, on a small sample. Rare profiles with production experience sit above that.

### What's the difference between an IT services firm and an AI agency?

An IT services firm mostly supplies people who reinforce your teams, usually on time and materials, under your management. An AI agency takes a project end to end with its own team and a deliverable. The line is blurry: check who owns the outcome in the contract.

### Can you start with a freelancer and move to an agency later?

Yes, if the code lives in your repository and is documented. The reverse works too: an agency ships and hands over, a freelancer maintains.

### Which option fits an AI software factory?

You need people who have already standardized tools, agents and skills across several teams, and who train your developers. That's rarely a job for a lone freelancer. An agency embedded in your teams, or a dedicated in-house team, fits better.
